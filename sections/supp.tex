\section{Implementation details}
\label{supp:impl}

\subsection{Kalman residual forecaster}
\label{supp:forecaster}

\paragraph{Forecast quantity and coordinates.}
The forecaster tracks the block-stack residual of the image tokens, $\res_i=\hout_i-\hin_i$, which for \flux at
$1024^2$ has 4096 tokens on a $64{\times}64$ grid and 3072 channels. In the DCT variant, every channel is transformed
with the orthonormal 2D DCT-II $\mathcal D$ on the token grid, and the coefficient with frequency indices $(u,v)$
belongs to band $\ell$ if its normalized radius $r=\sqrt{(u/64)^2+(v/64)^2}/\sqrt2$ lies in $[e_\ell,e_{\ell+1})$, with
edges $\{0,\frac1{64},\frac1{32},\frac1{16},\frac18,\frac14,\frac12,1\}$ (seven bands, \cref{tab:supp-calib}). The
coefficients of band $\ell$ are divided by $\varsigma_\ell$, where $\varsigma^2_\ell$ is the calibrated mean power of the
residual's coefficients in that band, so that the filter operates in relative units; this scaling cancels in the correction
(\cref{supp:smoother}). The per-element variant omits the DCT and uses a single band whose scale is derived from the mean of
the seven calibrated powers.

\paragraph{Time coordinate.}
Following SpecKCast~\cite{rajabi2026speckcast}, every band has its own time coordinate derived from the noise schedule.
With the log signal-to-noise ratio $\lambda_i=2\log((1-\sigma_i)/\sigma_i)$ ($\sigma_i$ clamped to
$[10^{-4},1-10^{-4}]$) and $t_\ell(i)=\mathrm{sigmoid}\big((\lambda_i+\log p_\ell)/T_w\big)$ with $T_w=2$,
\begin{equation}
  s_\ell(i)=2\,\frac{t_\ell(i)-t_\ell(0)}{t_\ell(N{-}1)-t_\ell(0)}-1
  \label{eq:supp-time}
\end{equation}
increases from $-1$ to $1$ over the $N=50$ steps, and $\ds_{\ell,i}=s_\ell(i)-s_\ell(i-1)$. Here $p_\ell$ is the calibrated power of the
clean-image estimate in band $\ell$: the clock of a band advances fastest when the signal-to-noise ratio of its
frequencies is close to one. The per-element variant uses the count-weighted mean of the $p_\ell$.

\paragraph{Filter.}
Each coefficient (DCT variant) or element (per-element variant) has the state $\kstate=[m,\nu]^\top$, and all
coefficients of a band share the covariance $\kP_\ell$, hence the Kalman and smoother gains. Prediction and update
follow \maineqref{eq:predict} and \maineqref{eq:update} of the main paper with $\ds=\ds_{\ell,i}$, $\tau=0.5$ and
$R=10^{-3}$. The filter predicts at every step and is updated only at computed steps. On skipped steps the cache writes
the predicted values, transformed back to residual units: $\hat\res_i=\mathcal D^{-1}(\prior{\mathbf m}_i\odot
\boldsymbol\varsigma)$, where the map $\boldsymbol\varsigma$ holds $\varsigma_\ell$ on the coefficients of band $\ell$.

\paragraph{Initialization.}
At the first computed step (step 0, which the gate always computes), $m=z_0$, $\nu=0$,
$\kP_\ell=\mathrm{diag}(R,\,2q_\ell)$ and $\kappa_\ell=1$, where $q_\ell$ is the calibrated process-noise intensity.

\paragraph{Process-noise adaptation.}
At a computed step $b$, before the update, the filter compares the mean squared innovation over all coefficients and
channels of band $\ell$, $\overline{e^2_{\ell,b}}$, with its predicted variance $S_{\ell,b}=[\prior{\kP}_{\ell,b}]_{00}+R$:
\begin{equation}
  \begin{split}
    \gamma_{\ell,b}&=\mathrm{clip}_{[0.1,10]}\big(\overline{e^2_{\ell,b}}/S_{\ell,b}\big),\\
    \kappa_\ell&\gets\mathrm{clip}_{[0.1,10]}\big(\kappa_\ell\,\gamma_{\ell,b}^{0.2}\big).
  \end{split}
  \label{eq:supp-adapt}
\end{equation}
The update at $b$ uses the gain computed before this change; the new $\kappa_\ell$ scales $\kQ$ from the next
prediction on~\cite{mehra1972approaches}.

\paragraph{Constants.}
$\tau=0.5$, $R=10^{-3}$, $T_w=2$, the exponent $0.2$ and the clipping ranges in \eqref{eq:supp-adapt} are the same for
all budgets and models. They were fixed on the development set; $\tau=0.1$ is the default of
SpecKCast~\cite{rajabi2026speckcast} (ablated in \cref{tab:ablation-full}, group (d)).

\subsection{Calibration}
\label{supp:calib}
The DCT variant is calibrated once for \flux from 16 uncached 50-step trajectories at $1024^2$ with guidance 3.5, one
per COCO caption~\cite{lin2014coco}; the 16 captions are disjoint from the 64 development and the 200 DrawBench prompts.
For a fixed random subset of 384 of the 3072 channels we record the block-stack residual at every step, together with
the latent input and the velocity output of the transformer. From these,
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item $\varsigma^2_\ell$ is the mean squared DCT coefficient of the residual in band $\ell$, over steps, channels and
  trajectories;
  \item $p_\ell$ is the mean power in band $\ell$ of the clean-image estimate $\hat\lat_0=\lat_i-\sigma_i\vel_i$ over
  the last 25 steps (latent DCT coefficients, grouped by the same physical frequencies as the token-grid bands);
  \item $q_\ell$ is, per trajectory, the median over steps of the mean squared change of the finite-difference trend
  $\nu_i=(z_{i+1}-z_i)/\ds_{\ell,i+1}$ (relative units) divided by the mean of the two adjacent band-time steps,
  averaged over the trajectories;
  \item the initial trend variance is $2q_\ell$.
\end{itemize}
\cref{tab:supp-calib} lists the values. No evaluation prompt is used for calibration.

\subsection{Per-element and calibration-free variants}
\label{supp:variants}
The \emph{per-element} variant uses the same calibration, reduced to one band (count-weighted means of $p_\ell$, $q_\ell$
and the initial trend variance; scale: square root of the mean of the $\varsigma^2_\ell$). The \emph{calibration-free} per-element variant needs no
trajectories: $q=7400$, initial trend variance $15000$, a time coordinate that is uniform in the step index (linear
from $-1$ to $1$), and as scale the root mean square of the first observed residual. All other constants are those of
\cref{supp:forecaster}. On \flux, both variants perform like the DCT filter once \pcgs is applied
(\cref{tab:ablation-full}, group (d), and \cref{tab:ablation-2x2}); we use the calibration-free variant for video
(\cref{supp:video}).

\subsection{Predictor-corrector gap smoothing}
\label{supp:pcgs-impl}

\paragraph{Bookkeeping.}
For each skipped step $i$ of the current gap, the cache keeps the latent input $\lat_i$, the conditioning $\cond_i$,
the forecast $\hat\res_i$ and the prior covariances $\prior{\kP}_{\ell,i}$ of all bands. At the update of the closing
computed step $b$ it records $\prior{\kP}_{\ell,b}$ and the innovation. The gains $g_{i,\ell}$ of
\maineqref{eq:smooth} are computed by the backward recursion $\kPhi(b,i)=\kPhi(b,i{+}1)\,\kA_{i+1}$ over the gap,
from quantities the filter maintains anyway (illustrative values: \cref{tab:supp-gains}).

\paragraph{Correction in residual space.}
For the DCT variant, $\resf_i=\hat\res_i+\mathcal D^{-1}\big(\mathbf G_i\odot\mathcal D(\res_b-\hat\res_b)\big)$, where the
gain map $\mathbf G_i$ has the value $g_{i,\ell}$ on all coefficients of band $\ell$ (derivation in
\cref{supp:smoother}); for the per-element variant $\mathbf G_i$ is the scalar $g_i$.

\paragraph{Re-integration, continuation and gate refresh.}
The gap is replayed from the latent that the sampler used at the first skipped step, $\lat_{a+1}$, with
\maineqref{eq:reint}: $\Emb$ (the patch embedding) and $\Out$ (final adaptive normalization and linear projection) are
evaluated once per skipped step, and each Euler step uses the arithmetic of the diffusers flow-matching scheduler (the
bf16 velocity times the fp32 step size is added to the fp32 copy of the bf16 latent, then cast to bf16). With the
identity smoother ($\resf_i=\hat\res_i$), the replay reproduces the uncorrected run of the per-element filter bitwise
(unit test on the GPU). At step $b$, the returned velocity is \maineqref{eq:offset}, computed in fp32 and cast to bf16.
The gate refresh recomputes the \sea gate's stored input of step $b$ from $\lat'_b$: one adaptive normalization of the
first block on $\Emb(\lat'_b)$ and one \sea filter. The last step is always computed; its gap is corrected in the same
way, and no refresh is needed.

\paragraph{Tests.}
In float64 on synthetic residuals with the \flux calibration and a gate-like schedule (9 gaps, 36 skipped steps), the
implemented gains agree with brute-force joint-Gaussian conditioning of the damped-trend model to a relative error of
$8\cdot10^{-16}$ (DCT bands) and $6\cdot10^{-16}$ (per element). With every step computed there is no gap, and the
images are bitwise equal to the 50-step reference.
% provenance: research_v2/ablations/ABLATIONS.md, unit tests T1 (tests/test_cpu_math.out), U1-U3 (tests/check_gpu_tests.out).

\paragraph{\nfe accounting and cost.}
\nfe is counted by forward pre-hooks on all 19 double-stream and 38 single-stream blocks (block calls / 57) and
cross-checked against the forward pass's own record of computed steps: every DrawBench run has zero mismatches and no
partial block stack, and \pcgs never calls a block. Per image, \ours evaluates $\Emb$ and $\Out$ 44.0 times at 18.1
\nfe and 47.0 times at 13 \nfe (skipped steps plus corrected computed steps; \cref{tab:supp-correction}), each about
3.4\,GFLOP (main paper, \maincref{sec:cost}), plus 11.2 and 9.0 gate refreshes, in total below 0.3\% of one transformer
evaluation. The peak allocated memory exceeds that of the uncached sampler by 0.16\,GiB (\kalsea: 0.14\,GiB; \seacache:
0.05\,GiB). Wall-clock measurements are pending (\cref{supp:cost}).

\section{Derivation of the gap smoother}
\label{supp:smoother}

\paragraph{Setting.}
Consider one Kalman filter of the predictor (one residual element, or one DCT band whose coefficients share the
covariance). Let $a<b$ be consecutive computed steps, $\mathcal Y_a$ the observations up to step $a$, and $a<i<b$ a
skipped step. No observation falls into the gap, so the predicted moments are propagated without update:
$\prior{\kstate}_{i}=\kA_i\prior{\kstate}_{i-1}$ and $\prior{\kP}_i=\kA_i\prior{\kP}_{i-1}\kA_i^\top+\kQ_i$ for
$a+1<i\le b$, starting from $\prior{\kstate}_{a+1}=\kA_{a+1}\post{\kstate}_a$. Define the transition
$\kPhi(b,i)=\kA_b\kA_{b-1}\cdots\kA_{i+1}$, with $\kPhi(b,b)=\mathbf I$, and the innovation variance
$S_b=\mathbf h^\top\prior{\kP}_b\mathbf h+R$ with $\mathbf h=[1,0]^\top$.

\paragraph{Proposition.}
For $a<i\le b$,
\begin{equation}
  \E\!\left[\kstate_i\mid\mathcal Y_a,z_b\right]=\prior{\kstate}_i+\prior{\kP}_i\kPhi(b,i)^\top\mathbf h\,S_b^{-1}e_b,
  \quad e_b=z_b-\mathbf h^\top\prior{\kstate}_b,
  \label{eq:supp-smooth}
\end{equation}
and its first component gives the gain $g_i=[\prior{\kP}_i\kPhi(b,i)^\top]_{00}/S_b$ of \maineqref{eq:smooth}.

\paragraph{Proof by conditioning.}
Iterating the state equation, $\kstate_b=\kPhi(b,i)\kstate_i+\mathbf w_{i\to b}$, where $\mathbf w_{i\to b}$ is a sum of
process-noise terms of steps $i+1,\dots,b$; it has zero mean and is independent of $\kstate_i$ and of $\mathcal Y_a$.
With $z_b=\mathbf h^\top\kstate_b+\eta_b$ and $\eta_b\sim\mathcal N(0,R)$ independent of everything else,
$\mathrm{Cov}(\kstate_i,z_b\mid\mathcal Y_a)=\prior{\kP}_i\kPhi(b,i)^\top\mathbf h$ and
$\mathrm{Var}(z_b\mid\mathcal Y_a)=S_b$. Conditioning the jointly Gaussian pair $(\kstate_i,z_b)$ given $\mathcal Y_a$
on $z_b$ gives \eqref{eq:supp-smooth}.

\paragraph{Equivalence with the RTS smoother.}
The RTS recursion~\cite{rauch1965maximum,sarkka2013bayesian} over $[a,b]$, using the observations up to $b$, reads
$\smooth{\kstate}_i=\post{\kstate}_i+\mathbf C_i(\smooth{\kstate}_{i+1}-\prior{\kstate}_{i+1})$ with
$\mathbf C_i=\post{\kP}_i\kA_{i+1}^\top(\prior{\kP}_{i+1})^{-1}$, initialized with the filtered estimate
$\smooth{\kstate}_b=\post{\kstate}_b=\prior{\kstate}_b+\prior{\kP}_b\mathbf h S_b^{-1}e_b$. Inside the gap,
$\post{\kstate}_i=\prior{\kstate}_i$ and $\post{\kP}_i=\prior{\kP}_i$. We show by backward induction that
$\smooth{\kstate}_i-\prior{\kstate}_i=\prior{\kP}_i\kPhi(b,i)^\top\mathbf h S_b^{-1}e_b$. The case $i=b$ holds because
$\kPhi(b,b)=\mathbf I$. If it holds for $i+1$, then
\begin{align*}
  \smooth{\kstate}_i-\prior{\kstate}_i
  &=\prior{\kP}_i\kA_{i+1}^\top(\prior{\kP}_{i+1})^{-1}\prior{\kP}_{i+1}\kPhi(b,i{+}1)^\top\mathbf h S_b^{-1}e_b\\
  &=\prior{\kP}_i\big(\kPhi(b,i{+}1)\kA_{i+1}\big)^\top\mathbf h S_b^{-1}e_b,
\end{align*}
and $\kPhi(b,i{+}1)\kA_{i+1}=\kPhi(b,i)$. \hfill$\square$

\paragraph{Consequences.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item At the newest observation the smoothed and filtered estimates coincide, so \pcgs leaves the predictor for the
  next gap unchanged (main paper, \maincref{sec:pcgs}, Remarks).
  \item \emph{Random walk.} For the model without trend ($\kA=1$, $\kQ=q\ds$), an exact observation at $a$
  ($\post{\kP}_a=0$) and $R\to0$, the gain is $g_i=(s_i-s_a)/(s_b-s_a)$, \ie, linear interpolation in the time
  coordinate. With the reuse forecast $\hat\res_i=\res_a$ this is the linear interpolation between $\res_a$ and $\res_b$
  used for reuse with \pcgs in the ablations.
  \item \emph{DCT variant.} Each band $\ell$ has its own $\kA$, $\kQ$ and $\kP$, hence its own gains $g_{i,\ell}$. The
  band normalization multiplies $z_b$, $\prior{m}_b$ and $\prior{m}_i$ by the same factor and cancels in
  \eqref{eq:supp-smooth}, which gives the residual-space correction of \cref{supp:pcgs-impl}.
  \item \emph{Gain shape.} \cref{tab:supp-gains} shows the gains for the gap 33--41 of the most common 13-\nfe
  schedule with $\kappa{=}1$: they rise from 0.04--0.11 at the first skipped step to 0.80--0.87 at the last one, and the
  highest-frequency bands rise most slowly.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Why smoothing minimizes the latent error to first order.}
Write $h_i=\sigma_{i+1}-\sigma_i$ and let $\boldsymbol\rho=(\boldsymbol\rho_{a+1},\dots,\boldsymbol\rho_{b-1})$ be the
residuals used to replay the gap, $\lat_{i+1}=\lat_i+h_i\Out(\Emb(\lat_i)+\boldsymbol\rho_i,\cond_i)$, from the exact
$\lat_{a+1}$. Let $\res=(\res_{a+1},\dots,\res_{b-1})$ be the residuals that full computation would produce and
$\lat^\star_b$ the corresponding latent. Linearizing the replay around the fully computed trajectory,
\begin{equation}
  \lat_b(\boldsymbol\rho)-\lat^\star_b\approx\sum_{i=a+1}^{b-1}h_i\,\mathbf M_{b,i}\,\mathbf J_i\,(\boldsymbol\rho_i-\res_i)
  =\mathbf L\,(\boldsymbol\rho-\res),
  \label{eq:supp-linear}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf J_i$ is the Jacobian of $\Out$ with respect to its first argument and
$\mathbf M_{b,i}=\prod_{j=i+1}^{b-1}(\mathbf I+h_j\mathbf J_j\mathbf E_j)$ propagates a latent perturbation from step
$i+1$ to $b$ ($\mathbf E_j$: Jacobian of $\Emb$, a linear map for \flux). For any fixed linear map $\mathbf L$ and any
estimate $\boldsymbol\rho$ that depends only on the observations $\mathcal Y$,
$\E[\|\mathbf L(\boldsymbol\rho-\res)\|^2\mid\mathcal Y]=\|\mathbf L(\boldsymbol\rho-\E[\res\mid\mathcal Y])\|^2+
\E[\|\mathbf L(\res-\E[\res\mid\mathcal Y])\|^2\mid\mathcal Y]$, which is minimal for
$\boldsymbol\rho=\E[\res\mid\mathcal Y]$. Under the filter's model, the conditional means given the observations up to
$b$ are the smoothed residuals $\resf_i$, whereas the forecasts $\hat\res_i$ are the conditional means given the
observations up to $a$. Hence, to first order and under the filter's model, re-integrating with $\resf_i$ minimizes the
expected squared error of $\lat'_b$ among all estimates available at step $b$. The argument covers the DCT variant
because the DCT is linear and the conditional mean is taken per coefficient. It does not cover model misspecification,
which is why simpler gains can do as well in practice (main paper, \maincref{sec:ablations}).

\label{supp:protocol}

\paragraph{Published results and the mapping from $\delta$ to \nfe.}
\seacache reports compute as TFLOPs (2976 for its 50-step original, \ie, 59.5 per transformer evaluation). We convert
its published rows to \nfe (\cref{tab:supp-published}); these are the rows marked $^\dagger$ in the main paper's
\maincref{tab:flux-main}. Its larger-budget row is labelled $\delta=0.3$, but its 1098 TFLOPs imply 18.45 evaluations.
With the released gate, $\delta=0.3$ computes 20.88 steps per image on average in our setup (20 or 21 per prompt), and
$\delta=0.37$ computes 18.14; its $\delta=0.6$ row (12.99) agrees with our measurement (13.00, on every prompt). We
cannot tell whether the TFLOPs or the threshold of the $\delta=0.3$ row are off. Running our cache at the \nfe implied by
the TFLOPs ($\delta=0.37$) is the conservative choice in both cases: if their run used 18.4 evaluations, the comparison is
matched; if it used 20.9, \seacache had more compute than our cache. Our re-run of \seacache reaches 26.89\,dB at
$\delta=0.3$, close to the published 26.29\,dB, and 25.33\,dB at $\delta=0.37$; at $\delta=0.6$ it reproduces the
published 21.33\,dB (21.26\,dB). The published numbers were obtained on different hardware (NVIDIA Blackwell Pro 6000,
according to the paper) and with seeds the paper does not state, so the main paper compares with them without
confidence intervals, and with our re-run with paired intervals (\cref{tab:supp-samesetup}). The published 25- and
15-step rows appear swapped (the 25-step row is worse on every metric); we cite them as published.

\paragraph{Matched schedules.}
Within one threshold, all our caches use the same \sea gate, but the gate is closed loop: its decisions depend on the
latent and hence on the filler. \cref{tab:supp-steps} counts the prompts on which two or three caches computed exactly
the same set of steps. At the thresholds of the main comparisons, \seacache, \kalsea and \ours computed the same steps on
\suppSameThreeA, \suppSameThreeC{} and \suppSameThreeB{} of the 200 prompts ($\delta=0.37$, $0.3$ and $0.6$), any two of
them on 183 to 196, and the per-prompt \nfe never differs by more than \suppSameThreeMaxDnfe. All ablation runs of
\cref{tab:ablation-full} are within 0.06 \nfe of \ours at $\delta=0.3$ and at exactly 13.00 \nfe at $\delta=0.6$.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
Images are compared with the uncached 50-step output of the same code path, prompt and seed (seed = DrawBench prompt
index, 200 prompts in the file order). PSNR: scikit-image on 8-bit RGB with data range 255. SSIM: scikit-image
structural similarity on 8-bit RGB (default $7{\times}7$ uniform window, channels averaged). LPIPS: AlexNet
features~\cite{zhang2018lpips}, inputs scaled to $[-1,1]$. \seacache does not state its SSIM implementation or LPIPS
backbone (TeaCache's evaluation code, on which it builds, uses a Gaussian $11{\times}11$ SSIM window), so published SSIM
and LPIPS values may not be exactly comparable with ours; PSNR on 8-bit RGB is unambiguous up to the data range. Confidence intervals: paired percentile
bootstrap over prompts, 10{,}000 resamples, with the same resampling indices for every pair with the same number of
prompts.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.}
The DCT filter accumulates its band statistics with atomic additions on the GPU (scatter-add), so two runs of the same
configuration are not bitwise identical; with deterministic algorithms enforced they are. On DrawBench, a second run
of \ours differs by \suppOursRerunA{} at $\delta=0.3$ and \suppOursRerunB{} at $\delta=0.6$
(\cref{tab:ablation-full}, caption), and two independent runs of \kalsea differ by \suppKalRerunA{} at $\delta=0.37$ and
\suppKalRerunB{} at $\delta=0.6$, with identical computed steps on \suppKalRerunSameA/200 and \suppKalRerunSameB/200 prompts (the main paper's \maincref{tab:flux-main} uses one of these runs, the $2{\times}2$ ablation of
\cref{tab:ablation-2x2} the other). On the development set, a same-session re-run of \kalsea differs by $-$0.05 and
$+$0.03\,dB (\cref{tab:supp-devset}). We therefore do not interpret differences below 0.1\,dB. The per-element filter,
reuse and the uncached reference regenerated bit-identical images on all 64 development prompts; for video, the
filter updates run with deterministic algorithms, and re-generated videos are bit-identical
(\cref{supp:video-repro}).

\section{What did not help}
\label{supp:negative}

\paragraph{Forecasting a sampler quantity instead of the residual.}
A forecaster can track a quantity of the sampler, the clean-image estimate $\hat\lat_0$, the noise estimate
$\hat{\boldsymbol\epsilon}$ or the velocity $\vel$, and reconstruct the velocity from the current latent on skipped
steps; for $\hat\lat_0$ and $\hat{\boldsymbol\epsilon}$ this feeds the latent's own drift back into the output. Open
loop, along uncached trajectories, the $\hat{\boldsymbol\epsilon}$ forecast is the most accurate
(\cref{tab:supp-openloop}). In closed-loop sampling, however, no target is better than the residual. On the development
set all differences are within 0.13\,dB with intervals that include zero (\cref{tab:supp-devabl}); on DrawBench with
DCT filters and without correction, the residual minus the target is $-$0.07\,dB [$-$0.23, 0.08] and $+$0.09\,dB
[$-$0.01, 0.20] for $\hat\lat_0$, $+$0.20\,dB [$-$0.08, 0.48] and $+$0.02\,dB [$-$0.13, 0.16] for
$\hat{\boldsymbol\epsilon}$, and $-$0.07\,dB [$-$0.22, 0.08] and $-$0.02\,dB [$-$0.12, 0.09] for $\vel$ (at 20.8 and
13 \nfe; absolute values in \cref{tab:ablation-full}, group (f)). A mixture of $\hat\lat_0$ and
$\hat{\boldsymbol\epsilon}$ with Wiener weights was not better either.
% provenance: research_v2/ablations/eval/eval_all.json (paired kalsea_<d> vs abl_tgt_*_<d>), ABLATIONS.md finding 8.

\paragraph{Using the innovation to decide when to compute.}
Instead of correcting the gap, the forecast error can set the length of the next gap: an error controller measured the
relative output error of the forecast at each computed step (one extra output-layer evaluation) and scaled the next gap
by $\mathrm{clip}_{[0.5,2]}\big((\epsilon_{\mathrm{tol}}/\mathrm{err})^{1/2}\big)$, replacing the \sea gate. On the
development set it raised PSNR over the \sea-gated per-element \kalsea at a similar mean \nfe ($+$2.76\,dB [1.75, 3.80]
at 13.36 vs.\ 13 \nfe), but the gain came from a different allocation of the computed steps rather than from adaptivity:
a fixed schedule with the controller's typical allocation, (0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 11, 16, 23, 31, 42, 49), was as good
as the controller ($-$0.26\,dB [$-$0.93, 0.44] for the controller relative to it, 30 prompts), and a predictive variant
driven by the filter covariance alone was much worse ($-$5.07\,dB [$-$6.01, $-$4.17] at 13 \nfe relative to \kalsea).
The per-image \nfe of the controller also varied (standard deviation 1.6--2.4), so its budget was matched only on
average. The fixed schedule was derived on the same development prompts and was not evaluated on DrawBench. Which steps
to compute is thus a separate question; all methods in this paper share \seacache's gate.
% provenance: research_v2/ablations/eval/eval_devset.txt (C1_pure_pe_t0.43, C2_fixedavg_pe_13, C2_pure_pe_t0.454,
% C2_pred_pe_t1.75), research_v2/techC/RESULT.md (controller rule), ABLATIONS.md A10.

\subsection{Protocol and implementation}
\label{supp:video-protocol}

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering

  \input{figures/fig_video_sweep_caption}

\paragraph{Protocol.}
We follow the video protocol of \seacache~\cite{chung2026seacache}: the 944 unique prompts of VBench's full prompt
list~\cite{huang2024vbench} (946 lines with two duplicates), one video per prompt, seed 42 for every prompt and method,
50 sampling steps, and fidelity to the uncached 50-step video of the same prompt and seed.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item HunyuanVideo~\cite{kong2024hunyuanvideo} (official code): $480{\times}640$, 65 frames, Euler flow sampler with
  shift 7 and reversed flow, embedded guidance 6, a single (guidance-distilled) pass per step, bf16; videos saved at
  24\,fps.
  \item Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B~\cite{wan2025wan} (official code): $832{\times}480$, 65 frames, UniPC~\cite{zhao2023unipc} with
  shift 5, classifier-free guidance~\cite{ho2022cfg} with scale 5 and the default negative prompt, bf16; 16\,fps.
  \seacache's text states 65 frames for both models, but its Wan2.1 compute corresponds to 81 frames: its 8214 TFLOPs
  for 50 steps (164.3 per step) match the linear-layer FLOPs of this model at $832{\times}480$ and 81 frames (164.2 per
  step, both guidance passes), whereas 65 frames give 133.0. Its published Wan2.1 results may thus come from 81-frame
  videos; we use the 65 frames of its text.
  % provenance: paper_plan/seacache_protocol_from_paper.md, Sec. 4.3 (FLOP convention that reproduces SeaCache's FLUX
  % TFLOPs: 2 x MACs of the linear layers; checks in paper_plan/seacache_protocol_checks.py).
  \item Gate: the official \sea gate of each model ($\beta=3$, filter over frames, height and width), first and last
  step always computed, thresholds passed as parameters. With the gate as released, our \seacache path reproduces the
  outputs of \seacache's released scripts bit for bit on our test prompts (both models). On HunyuanVideo, every gated
  method reads the sampler's noise level (\cref{supp:video-gate}).
  \item \nfe: full transformer evaluations per video, counted by forward hooks on every block and checked against the
  gate's record (no mismatch in any video). On Wan2.1, the conditional and unconditional passes keep separate gate
  accumulators, but their gate distances were identical in every video, so both passes computed the same steps; a
  computed step runs both passes and counts once.
  \item Metrics: PSNR, SSIM and LPIPS on the decoded MP4 frames, as in TeaCache's video metric
  code~\cite{liu2025teacache}: PSNR on 8-bit RGB per frame (capped at 100\,dB), SSIM with an $11{\times}11$ Gaussian
  window ($\sigma=1.5$) averaged over the RGB channels, and LPIPS with AlexNet features on inputs in $[-1,1]$; frames
  are averaged per video, then videos. Paired 95\% bootstrap intervals over videos (10{,}000 resamples, seed 0).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Ports of the forecaster and of \pcgs.}
Both models use the calibration-free per-element filter (\cref{supp:variants}), one filter per guidance branch (one for
HunyuanVideo, two for Wan2.1), updated under deterministic algorithms so that runs are reproducible. HunyuanVideo
samples with Euler steps, so \pcgs applies as for \flux, with the patch embedding and the final layer as $\Emb$ and
$\Out$. For Wan2.1, whose UniPC solver is multistep, the solver state is saved after each computed step $a$; when step
$b$ closes the gap, both branches are smoothed, the state is restored and the solver steps $a{+}1,\dots,b{-}1$ are
replayed with the corrected guided outputs $\mathbf u_i'+w(\mathbf c_i'-\mathbf u_i')$, where $\mathbf c_i'$ and
$\mathbf u_i'$ are obtained from the patch embedding of the replayed latent, the smoothed residual of the branch and the
output head, and $w$ is the guidance scale. Sampling then continues from the replayed latent and solver state, with the
step-$b$ outputs recomputed from the corrected latent and the fresh residuals. In our tests on both models, the identity
smoother reproduces the uncorrected forecaster bitwise, and for Wan2.1 a corrected replay equals a fresh UniPC run with
the corrected outputs bitwise.
% provenance: video_logs/wan_cache_tests/real65_check.txt, unipc_replay_rerun.txt; video_logs/hv_cache_test_real.json
% (kalsea_pcgs_identity_0.35 == kalsea_0.35), hv_cache_test2/compare.jsonl; video_code/*/cache_methods.py docstrings.

\subsection{HunyuanVideo gate}
\label{supp:video-gate}
The released \seacache script for HunyuanVideo binds the scheduler that exists when the model is loaded (noise levels
from 1 to 0 in steps of $1/1000$) to the gate, while sampling creates a new scheduler with the shifted 50-step
schedule. Its \sea filter therefore uses $\sigma=1-i/1000$ at step $i$, a noise level near one at every step, instead
of the sampling schedule. This matters most at step~1, whose gate distance is \suppVidDOneRel{} over the
\suppVidStepN{} prompts with the stale noise level and \suppVidDOneLive{} with the sampler's. With the released gate,
step~1 is thus skipped on most prompts once $\delta\gtrsim0.21$: on \suppVidSkipAtPub{} prompts at \seacache's
published $\delta=0.19$, on \suppVidSkipAtTwoOne{} at $\delta=0.21$ and on all of them above \suppVidDOneRelMax.
Skipping it is
costly: at $\delta=\suppVidStepDelta$ (\suppVidRelHiNfe{} \nfe), the \suppVidStepSkip{} prompts that skip step~1 score
\suppVidStepCostSea\,dB (\seacache) and \suppVidStepCostOurs\,dB (\ours) below the others, and the corrected gate raises
their PSNR by \suppVidStepFixSea{} and \suppVidStepFixOurs\,dB at the same budget (others: \suppVidStepFixSeaComp{} and
\suppVidStepFixOursComp\,dB). We therefore let the gate of every gated method (\seacache, \ours and all ablations) read
the noise levels of the sampling schedule; at step~0 of each of the \suppVidLiveCheckN{} videos of the main comparison,
we checked that the gate's noise levels equal the sampler's. The re-integration of \pcgs always uses the sampling
schedule. \cref{tab:supp-video-gate} compares the two gates on all prompts at matched \nfe: the correction raises the
PSNR of both methods by several dB, and \ours improves on \seacache with either gate, more with the corrected one.

\subsection{Ablations and budget sweep}
\label{supp:video-abl}

\paragraph{Ablations.}
On 20 prompts per model, drawn at random (stratified by VBench dimension) for our pilot runs before the full runs, we
repeat the ablations of the main paper's \maincref{tab:ablation} that concern the corrector and the predictor
(\cref{tab:supp-video-abl}). All variants share the gate, threshold and \nfe of \ours. These prompts are not held out:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item The shape of the gains hardly matters: linear interpolation from $\res_a$ to $\res_b$ instead of the RTS gains

  \suppVidAblLinWB{} on Wan2.1.

  (\suppVidAblStateWA{} and \suppVidAblStateWB).

\subsection{Reproducibility}
\label{supp:video-repro}
The video runs are deterministic. For \suppVidDetRuns{} configurations of both models, the full runs regenerated the 20
prompts of an earlier pilot run and reproduced its videos bit for bit (\suppVidDetVideos{} videos: MP4 files, decoded
frames and computed steps), in part after code changes in between. After the ablation options were added to the code,
the configurations of the main comparison (\seacache and \ours at both budgets of both models, one prompt each,
\suppVidDetAfter{} videos) again reproduced the videos of the full runs bit for bit. Differences between video methods
are therefore not run-to-run noise; their intervals reflect the variability over prompts only.
