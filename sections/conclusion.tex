\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Caching fillers for diffusion transformers are open loop: the errors of skipped steps are integrated into the latent
and never corrected. \pcgs closes this loop at no cost in network evaluations: once the next step is computed, it
estimates the residuals of the skipped gap with the RTS smoother of the forecaster's own state-space model and
re-integrates the gap through the output layers. With the \sea gate and a Kalman residual forecaster, it improves
fidelity to the 50-step sampler at every budget we tested on \flux. The benefit lies in the re-integration itself: a
linear interpolation of the residual across the gap works as well as the RTS gains, and the correction also repairs
reuse and linear extrapolation. Video: \tbd. Promising directions are to choose the computed steps jointly
with the corrector and to correct caches that operate on features inside the block stack.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Matched \nfe is not matched latency: \pcgs adds output-layer evaluations (about 0.3\% of one transformer evaluation per
image) and 0.02\,GiB of memory over \kalsea, and the filter adds elementwise work on every step. Our metrics measure
fidelity to the 50-step sampler, the goal of a cache, not sample quality. Runs are not bitwise reproducible (up to
0.04\,dB between two runs). Our image results cover a single model. At a computed step, \pcgs attaches the residual
evaluated at the uncorrected latent to the corrected one, an approximation that is accurate only for small
corrections; at the smallest budget, with gaps of up to twelve steps, its gain is modest (0.31\,dB at 9 \nfe).
