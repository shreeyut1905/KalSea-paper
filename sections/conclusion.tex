\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Caching fillers for diffusion transformers are open loop. \pcgs closes this loop at no cost in network evaluations: once
the next step is computed, it estimates the residuals of the skipped gap with the RTS smoother of the forecaster's own
state-space model and re-integrates the gap through the output layers. With the \sea gate and a Kalman residual
forecaster, it improves fidelity to the 50-step sampler at every budget we tested on \flux, and the gains carry over to
HunyuanVideo and Wan2.1. The
benefit lies in the re-integration itself: a linear interpolation across the gap works as well as the RTS gains, and
the correction also repairs reuse and linear extrapolation. Promising directions are to choose the computed steps jointly
with the corrector and to correct caches that operate on features inside the block stack.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Matched \nfe is not matched latency: \pcgs adds output-layer evaluations (\cref{sec:cost}) and 0.02\,GiB over
\kalsea, and the filter adds elementwise work: at equal \nfe, \ours is 3--5\% slower than
\seacache on \flux and at most 1.4\% on video.
% cost_measure (one prompt per configuration, B200): FLUX 2.12 vs 2.05 s (18 NFE), 1.60 vs 1.53 s (13); HunyuanVideo
% 38.6 vs 38.4 s, 27.5 vs 27.4 s; Wan2.1 26.5 vs 26.1 s, 19.4 vs 19.1 s (figures/src/data/cost.json).
Our metrics measure fidelity to the 50-step sampler, the goal of a cache, not sample quality. Our image results cover a single model.
% Wan2.1: full set +0.33 dB [0.16, 0.49] at 17 NFE (fr_metrics.json); 20-prompt sweep -1.69 dB [-2.37, -1.07] at 10 NFE
% (supplement, fig_video_sweep; paper_plan/video_ablations_20.json).
On Wan2.1, the same-setup PSNR gain is only 0.33\,dB at 17 \nfe and negative at 10 \nfe on 20 prompts (supplement). \pcgs attaches the
residual of the uncorrected latent to the corrected one, accurate only for small corrections; with gaps of up to
twelve steps its gain is modest (0.31\,dB at 9 \nfe).
