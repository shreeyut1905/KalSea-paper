\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Models and protocol.}
We follow the protocol of \seacache~\cite{chung2026seacache}. For text-to-image generation we use
\flux~\cite{blackforestlabs2024flux} at $1024{\times}1024$ with 50 Euler steps of its default flow-matching schedule,
embedded guidance 3.5, and the 200 DrawBench prompts~\cite{saharia2022imagen}; the seed of each image is its prompt
index. For text-to-video generation we use HunyuanVideo~\cite{kong2024hunyuanvideo} at $480{\times}640$ and
Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B~\cite{wan2025wan} at $832{\times}480$, both with 65 frames, 50 steps and seed 42, on the 944
unique VBench prompts~\cite{huang2024vbench}. All methods share the seeds, and each accelerated sample is compared with
the output of the uncached 50-step sampler for the same prompt and seed.

\paragraph{Budgets and baselines.}
We report the mean number of block-stack evaluations per image (\nfe, of 50), counted by hooks on every block, and
compare only at matched \nfe. Every baseline runs in our setup with the same prompts and seeds: TeaCache, TaylorSeer,
Spectrum~\cite{han2026spectrum}, SADA~\cite{jiang2025sada} and fewer sampling steps with their released code, and
\seacache with the same gate as our caches. Our caches use the
official \sea gate, whose threshold $\delta$ sets the budget. At $\delta=0.6$ it computes 13.0 steps both in
\seacache's published table and in our setup, where \seacache reproduces the published PSNR (21.26
vs.\ 21.33\,dB); at $\delta=0.3$ it computes 20.9 steps in our setup but 18.4 according to the published TFLOPs, so
we match \nfe rather than $\delta$ and run our caches at $\delta=0.37$ (18.1 \nfe) and $0.6$. For controlled comparisons (same code, seeds and hardware), four fillers run
behind the same gate: reuse (\seacache), linear extrapolation of the residual from the last two computed steps (the
first-order forecast of TaylorSeer~\cite{liu2025taylorseer}), the Kalman forecast (\kalsea), and \ours. Behind the same gate, the
caches compute the same steps on most prompts (\suppref{supp:protocol}).

\paragraph{Metrics.}
PSNR on 8-bit RGB, SSIM~\cite{wang2004ssim} (scikit-image, $7{\times}7$ window) and LPIPS (AlexNet
features~\cite{zhang2018lpips}) are computed against the 50-step reference and averaged over prompts. Differences
between methods run in the same setup come with paired bootstrap 95\% confidence intervals over prompts (10{,}000
resamples, percentile) and the fraction of prompts on which a method is better.

\paragraph{Development and test prompts.}
All design decisions (the smoother, the re-integration and continuation rules, $\tau$ and $R$) were made on 64 COCO
captions~\cite{lin2014coco} and the forecaster was calibrated on 16 others, so DrawBench is a held-out test set
(development-set results: \suppref{supp:devset}).

\paragraph{Implementation.}
For \flux, our cache replaces the transformer's forward pass in diffusers and leaves the scheduler unchanged. All
runs use bf16 on NVIDIA B200 GPUs. For video we use the calibration-free per-element filter.

\subsection{Comparison at matched \nfe}
\label{sec:main-results}

\cref{tab:flux-main} compares \ours with all baselines in our setup. At 18.1 \nfe, \ours reaches 27.72\,dB PSNR,
2.39\,dB [2.03, 2.75] above \seacache at the same \nfe (better on 88\% of the prompts) and 2.2\,dB above Spectrum,
with the best SSIM and LPIPS at this budget; it is also above the 26.29\,dB that \seacache reports at 18.4 \nfe. At
20.8 \nfe it reaches 29.62\,dB and exceeds every baseline that uses more evaluations, including SADA's default
configuration at 24.0 \nfe (29.41\,dB). At 13 \nfe, it improves over \seacache by 1.27\,dB [1.02, 1.52] (79\%;
22.53 vs.\ 21.26\,dB, reported: 21.33\,dB) and over TeaCache, TaylorSeer and fewer steps, but Spectrum is 1.2\,dB
higher; its fixed schedule always computes the first five steps, which the \sea gate partly skips at $\delta=0.6$. ImageReward and CLIP score stay at the level of the uncached sampler, so the gain in fidelity does not
come at a cost in perceived quality.

\paragraph{Budget sweep.}
\cref{fig:tradeoff} varies $\delta$ from 0.2 to 1.0 in the same setup. \ours attains the highest PSNR and the
lowest LPIPS at every budget; its PSNR gain over \seacache grows from 0.74\,dB [0.46, 1.04] at 11 \nfe
to 3.89\,dB [3.49, 4.28] at 27 \nfe, and at 9 \nfe only SSIM and LPIPS improve significantly (+0.009 [0.003, 0.015]; $-$0.043 [$-$0.053, $-$0.033]).

\label{sec:video}

% gate analysis: paper_plan/hv_gate_fix.md and the released-gate full runs (hunyuan eval/full/fr_metrics.json).
% Step 1 with the released gate (supplement; data/video_supp.json, "step1"): skipped on 780 of 944 prompts at delta 0.21
% and on all above 0.222; at delta 0.207 the 559 prompts that skip it score 12.4 (SeaCache) / 14.0 dB (KalSea) lower.
We apply our cache with the per-element filter to HunyuanVideo and Wan2.1-1.3B at \seacache's two budgets
(\cref{tab:video-main}); on Wan2.1 (UniPC, classifier-free guidance) \pcgs replays the solver (\cref{sec:pcgs}).
\seacache's released HunyuanVideo script gates on a stale noise level near one, which skips step~1 once
$\delta\gtrsim0.21$ and costs 12--14\,dB on those prompts (\suppref{supp:video-gate}), so every gated method reads the sampler's noise
level, with $\delta$ set for 24.0 and 16.0 \nfe.
In our setup, \seacache then reproduces its published result at 24 \nfe (32.43 vs.\ 32.39\,dB) but not at 16.4
(24.53\,dB at 16.0 vs.\ 26.46\,dB; its released gate gives 20.52\,dB). All rows of \cref{tab:video-main} are run in
our setup. On HunyuanVideo, \ours improves on \seacache by 3.17\,dB [2.99, 3.35] at 24 \nfe and by 1.92\,dB
[1.70, 2.14] at 16 \nfe (better on 90\% and 72\% of the prompts), with better SSIM and LPIPS (examples:
\cref{fig:qual-models}); on Wan2.1 the paired gains are 2.27\,dB [2.13, 2.42] and 0.33\,dB [0.16, 0.49], the latter
with clearly better SSIM ($+$0.023) and LPIPS ($-$0.029). \ours also leads every other baseline at both budgets, by 8.3
and 0.2\,dB over the strongest on HunyuanVideo (Spectrum) and by 8.8 and 2.8\,dB on Wan2.1 (TeaCache at 25 \nfe,
Spectrum at 19), although several of them use more evaluations. On VBench, every method stays within about one point
of the uncached sampler; \ours scores above it on HunyuanVideo (81.11 and 81.19 vs.\ 81.02) and at the smaller Wan2.1
budget (80.74 vs.\ 80.56), and 0.2 below it at the larger one (80.36); it scores above \seacache at three of the four
budgets, so the gains in fidelity come without a loss in VBench quality.

\paragraph{Anatomy of the corrector.}
\cref{tab:ablation} reports paired PSNR differences to \ours at $\delta=0.3$ (20.8 \nfe) and $0.6$; differences below
0.1\,dB are within run-to-run noise (\suppref{supp:protocol}). Without \pcgs, PSNR drops by 0.83 and 0.61\,dB (Kalman row); across budgets, the predictor alone
loses its advantage over reuse as gaps grow, while \pcgs improves on it at every budget (\suppref{supp:results}).
The gain comes from re-integrating the latent: feeding the smoothed residuals only into the
filter state is no better than no correction ($-$0.86 vs.\ $-$0.83\,dB at 20.8 \nfe). Simpler gain shapes, such as a linear interpolation from $\res_a$ to $\res_b$, do as well as the RTS gains
(\suppref{supp:results}). The batched re-integration on the stored inputs $\hin_i$ loses 0.22\,dB [0.09, 0.34] at
20.8 \nfe, and the gate refresh has no measurable effect.

\paragraph{Other forecasters and fillers.}
With \pcgs, the forecaster's parametrization hardly matters: the per-element filter, a shorter damping $\tau=0.1$
and a calibration-free filter all stay within 0.1\,dB of \ours, so the two variants perform the same: we keep the DCT
variant on \flux, whose bands follow the frequency model of the \sea gate, and use the calibration-free per-element
filter for video, where it needs no calibration. Nor is \pcgs tied to a Kalman forecaster: it improves
reuse by 1.69 and 0.74\,dB and linear extrapolation by 1.15 and 0.80\,dB (\suppref{supp:results}); linear extrapolation with \pcgs
then ties \ours, while reuse with \pcgs stays 1.03 and 0.53\,dB below.
