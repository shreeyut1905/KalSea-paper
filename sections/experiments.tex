\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Models and protocol.}
We follow the protocol of \seacache~\cite{chung2026seacache}. For text-to-image generation we use
\flux~\cite{blackforestlabs2024flux} at $1024{\times}1024$ with 50 Euler steps of its default flow-matching schedule,
embedded guidance 3.5, and the 200 DrawBench prompts~\cite{saharia2022imagen}; the seed of each image is its prompt
index. For text-to-video generation we use HunyuanVideo~\cite{kong2024hunyuanvideo} at $480{\times}640$ and
Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B~\cite{wan2025wan} at $832{\times}480$, both with 65 frames, 50 steps and seed 42, on the 944
unique VBench prompts~\cite{huang2024vbench}. All methods share the seeds, and each accelerated sample is compared with
the output of the uncached 50-step sampler for the same prompt and seed.

\paragraph{Budgets and baselines.}
We report the mean number of block-stack evaluations per image (\nfe, of 50), counted by hooks on every block, and
compare only at matched \nfe. \cref{tab:flux-main} lists the results that \seacache published for itself, TeaCache,
TaylorSeer, $\Delta$-DiT, ToCa and fewer steps~\cite{chung2026seacache}, with TFLOPs converted to \nfe. Our caches
use the official \sea gate, whose threshold $\delta$ sets the budget. At $\delta=0.6$ it computes 13.0 steps both in
\seacache's table and in our setup, where our re-run of \seacache reproduces the published PSNR (21.26 vs.\
21.33\,dB); at $\delta=0.3$ it computes 20.9 steps in our setup but 18.4 according to the TFLOPs, so we run our cache
at $\delta=0.37$ (18.1 \nfe) and $0.6$. For controlled comparisons (same code, seeds and hardware), four fillers run
behind the same gate: reuse (\seacache), linear extrapolation of the residual from the last two computed steps (the
first-order forecast of TaylorSeer~\cite{liu2025taylorseer}), the Kalman forecast (\kalsea), and \ours. Behind the same gate, the
caches compute the same steps on most prompts (supplement).

\paragraph{Metrics.}
PSNR on 8-bit RGB, SSIM~\cite{wang2004ssim} (scikit-image, $7{\times}7$ window) and LPIPS (AlexNet
features~\cite{zhang2018lpips}) are computed against the 50-step reference and averaged over prompts. Differences
between methods run in the same setup come with paired bootstrap 95\% confidence intervals over prompts (10{,}000
resamples, percentile) and the fraction of prompts on which a method is better.

\paragraph{Development and test prompts.}
All design decisions (the smoother, the re-integration and continuation rules, $\tau$ and $R$) were made on a
development set of 64 COCO captions~\cite{lin2014coco}, and the forecaster was calibrated on 16 other COCO captions.
DrawBench was only used to score finished configurations and is thus a held-out test set (development-set results:
supplement).

\paragraph{Implementation.}
For \flux, our cache replaces the forward pass of the transformer in diffusers; the scheduler is unchanged, and the
re-integration \eqref{eq:reint} reproduces its arithmetic. All runs use bf16 on NVIDIA B200 GPUs. For video we use the
calibration-free per-element filter, since the DCT bands are defined on 2D token grids.

\subsection{Comparison at matched \nfe}
\label{sec:main-results}

\cref{tab:flux-main} compares \ours with published results. At 18.1 \nfe, \ours reaches 27.72\,dB PSNR, 1.43\,dB
above the 26.29\,dB reported for \seacache at 18.4 \nfe, with better SSIM and LPIPS, and it exceeds all baselines that
use more evaluations. At 13 \nfe, it reaches 22.53 vs.\ 21.33\,dB, again with better SSIM and LPIPS. In our same-setup
runs, \ours improves over \seacache by 2.39\,dB
[2.03, 2.75] (better on 88\% of the prompts) at 18.1 \nfe and by 1.27\,dB [1.02, 1.52] (79\%) at 13 \nfe, and \pcgs
adds 0.81\,dB [0.63, 1.00] and 0.61\,dB [0.48, 0.76] to \kalsea. The median per-prompt gain of \pcgs is 0.57 and
0.44\,dB; on 20\% and 22\% of the prompts it lowers PSNR, by at most 4.8 and 2.3\,dB.

\paragraph{Budget sweep.}
\cref{fig:tradeoff} varies $\delta$ from 0.2 to 1.0 in the same setup. \ours attains the highest PSNR and the
lowest LPIPS at every budget; its PSNR gain over our re-run of \seacache grows from 0.74\,dB [0.46, 1.04] at 11 \nfe
to 3.89\,dB [3.49, 4.28] at 27 \nfe, and at 9 \nfe only LPIPS improves significantly (0.043 [0.033, 0.053]). As the
open-loop argument predicts, the predictor alone loses its advantage over reuse as gaps grow (2.37\,dB at 27 \nfe,
0.11\,dB [$-$0.14, 0.34] at 11), and linear extrapolation falls below reuse at 11 and 9 \nfe, whereas \pcgs improves on
the predictor at every budget, by 0.31\,dB [0.24, 0.39] at 9 \nfe up to 1.52\,dB [1.30, 1.75] at 27 \nfe.

\paragraph{Qualitative results.}
The supplement compares the images of prompts selected by a fixed rule (lower quartile, median and upper quartile of the
per-prompt gain): with the same computed steps, reuse alters high-frequency structure, both forecasts restore it, and
\pcgs is usually closest to the reference. \cref{fig:collage} shows images generated by \ours at 27 \nfe.

% Step 1 with the released gate (supplement; data/video_supp.json, "step1"): skipped on 780 of 944 prompts at delta 0.21
% and on all above 0.222; at delta 0.207 the 559 prompts that skip it score 12.4 (SeaCache) / 14.0 dB (Ours) lower.
We apply our cache with the per-element filter to HunyuanVideo and Wan2.1-1.3B at \seacache's two budgets
(\cref{tab:video-main}); on Wan2.1 (UniPC, classifier-free guidance) \pcgs replays the solver (\cref{sec:pcgs}).
\seacache's released HunyuanVideo script binds its gate to a scheduler that the pipeline replaces at
inference, so the \sea filter sees a noise level near one at every step and skips step~1 once $\delta\gtrsim0.21$; prompts
that skip it score 12--14\,dB lower (supplement). We let every gated method read the sampler's noise level and set $\delta$ for 24.0 and
16.0 \nfe.
Our re-run of \seacache then reproduces its published result at 24 \nfe (32.43 vs.\ 32.39\,dB) but not at 16.4
(24.53\,dB at 16.0 vs.\ 26.46\,dB; its released gate gives 20.52\,dB), so \cref{tab:video-main} reports the re-run for
HunyuanVideo. There, \ours improves on \seacache by 3.17\,dB [2.99, 3.35] at 24 \nfe and by 1.92\,dB [1.70, 2.14]
at 16 \nfe (better on 90\% and 72\% of the prompts), with better SSIM and LPIPS. On Wan2.1 it exceeds \seacache's
published results by 5.07 and 2.07\,dB at 24 and 17 \nfe. In our setup the paired gains are 2.27\,dB [2.13, 2.42] and
0.33\,dB [0.16, 0.49], the latter with clearly better SSIM ($+$0.023) and LPIPS ($-$0.029); part of the distance to the
published Wan2.1 numbers thus comes from the setup (our clips have 65 frames).

\paragraph{Anatomy of the corrector.}
\cref{tab:ablation} reports paired PSNR differences to \ours at $\delta=0.3$ (20.8 \nfe) and $0.6$; differences below
0.1\,dB are within run-to-run noise (a second run of \ours differs by at most 0.04\,dB, as the filter's band statistics
use atomic GPU additions). The gain comes from re-integrating the latent: feeding the smoothed residuals only into the
filter state is no better than no correction ($-$0.86 vs.\ $-$0.83\,dB at 20.8 \nfe). The shape of the gains matters
little: a linear interpolation from $\res_a$ to $\res_b$ and the forecast plus a linearly weighted innovation match the
RTS gains at 13 \nfe and exceed them by 0.14\,dB [0.04, 0.25] at 20.8 \nfe; we keep the RTS gains chosen on the
development set (supplement). The batched re-integration on the stored inputs $\hin_i$ loses 0.22\,dB [0.09, 0.34] at
20.8 \nfe, and the gate refresh has no measurable effect.

\paragraph{Other forecasters and fillers.}
With \pcgs, the forecaster's parametrization hardly matters: the per-element filter, the damping $\tau=0.1$ of
SpecKCast and a calibration-free filter all stay within 0.1\,dB of \ours, so the frequency decomposition is unnecessary
for the residual and we use the per-element filter for video. Nor is \pcgs tied to a Kalman forecaster: it improves
reuse by 1.69 and 0.74\,dB and linear extrapolation by 1.15 and 0.80\,dB (supplement); linear extrapolation with \pcgs
then ties \ours, while reuse with \pcgs stays 1.03 and 0.53\,dB below. Forecasting a sampler quantity (clean image,
noise or velocity) instead of the residual is not better (supplement).
