\caption{\textbf{Overview of \ours.}
\textbf{(a)}~One sampler step. The \sea gate of \seacache accumulates the filtered relative change $\gdist_i$ of the
first block's modulated input and runs the block stack $\blocks$ only when the sum reaches~$\delta$. On a computed
step, the residual $\res_i$ updates a damped-trend Kalman filter for each DCT coefficient (the predictor); on a skipped
step, the filter's forecast $\hat\res_i$ takes its place. $\Emb$ and $\Out$ run at every step.
\textbf{(b)}~When a computed step~$b$ closes the gap $a{+}1,\dots,b{-}1$, \pcgs distributes the innovation $e_b$ over
the gap with the RTS gains $g_i$, which grow toward~$b$, and the smoothed residuals $\smooth{m}_i$ replace the
forecasts $\prior{m}_i$.
\textbf{(c)}~Re-integrating the gap from $\lat_{a+1}$ with $\Emb$ and $\Out$ only reduces the deviation of the latent
from full computation at~$b$ by 25--59\% in all ten gaps of this generation (shown: $5.0\%\to2.4\%$ and
$7.8\%\to3.2\%$), without extra transformer evaluations.
Panels (b) and (c) show one \flux generation at $\delta=0.6$ for the DrawBench prompt ``McDonalds Church'', chosen
because its PSNR gains (forecast over reuse, and gap smoothing over forecast) are closest to the benchmark averages.
The residual is projected onto a fixed random unit direction~$\mathbf{p}$ in the lowest DCT band, which holds 89\% of
its energy. Gray: full computation, run only for this figure.}
\label{fig:method}
